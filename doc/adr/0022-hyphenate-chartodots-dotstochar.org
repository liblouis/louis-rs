* ACCEPTED ADR-0022: Wait with ~lou_hyphenate~, ~lou_charToDots~ and ~lou_dotsToChar~, and where they go when they come
- Deciders :: CE
- Date :: [2026-11-12 Thu]

** Context and Problem Statement

liblouis has three entry points besides translation that louis-rs has
no counterpart for:

- ~lou_hyphenate~ :: where a word may be broken across lines, from the
  hyphenation dictionary (~.dic~) the table names. With a ~mode~, the
  word is braille and is hyphenated by way of its text. A braille
  formatter needs it to break lines; liblouisutdml is the typical
  user.
- ~lou_charToDots~ :: characters to braille cells, one by one, through
  the display table and without translating: braille that is already
  in some encoding, such as a BRF file, as dot patterns.
- ~lou_dotsToChar~ :: the reverse: dot patterns to the characters of
  the display encoding, typically to write BRF.

The fourth such entry point, ~lou_getEmphClasses~, has a counterpart:
~Translator::emphasis_classes~, which the name-based ~EmphasisSpan~
needs.

The machinery for all three exists: ~src/hyphenation.rs~ reads the
~.dic~ files, as ~nocross~ rules need them, and a ~BrailleEncoding~
holds the display rules. Nobody has asked for any of them yet. Should
louis-rs offer them now, and if so, how?

** Decision Drivers

- An API that is offered has to be kept stable, so it should be shaped
  by a real use.
- In liblouis the two conversions take a table list, because the
  display table is found in the table list. In louis-rs a braille
  encoding is a value of its own, ~BrailleEncoding~ (see
  [[file:0019-library-cli-workspace-split.org][ADR-0019]]).
- The shape of ~hyphenate~ depends on what its caller needs: text or
  braille, a word or a line, break points or the hyphenated string.
  Its main user would be a braille formatter
  ([[file:0017-braille-formatter.org][ADR-0017]], a draft).

** Considered Options

1. Offer all three now, as liblouis does: on ~Translator~, or as free
   functions taking tables.
2. Offer the two conversions now, on ~BrailleEncoding~, and
   ~hyphenate~ now on ~Translator~.
3. Wait for a user, and decide now where they will go.

** Decision Outcome

Option 3. None of them is built until someone needs it. When they
are:

- ~charToDots~ and ~dotsToChar~ become methods of ~BrailleEncoding~,
  e.g. ~encoding.to_dots(&str)~ and ~encoding.to_chars(&str)~: they
  are the encoding's two directions, applied on their own. Not on
  ~Translator~, which would tie them to translation tables as in
  liblouis.
- ~hyphenate~ comes with the braille formatter, or with the first
  other user, shaped by what that user needs.

*** Positive Consequences

- No API to keep stable before it has a user.
- Whoever adds the conversions later puts them where the encoding is,
  not where liblouis has them.

*** Negative Consequences

- An application moving off liblouis that uses one of the three has
  no replacement in louis-rs yet.

*** Migration path

None yet ([[file:0018-parity-or-migration-path.org][ADR-0018]]): an
application that needs one of the three should say so, and it can be
added as decided here. Each is small (S) once its user is known.

** Pros and Cons of the Options

*** Option 1: all three now, like liblouis

- Good, because a liblouis user finds what they know.
- Bad, because ~charToDots~ and ~dotsToChar~ would take translation
  tables only to find the display rules in them, which louis-rs
  deliberately does not do.
- Bad, because ~hyphenate~ would be shaped without its user.

*** Option 2: the conversions on ~BrailleEncoding~, ~hyphenate~ on ~Translator~, now

- Good, because the conversions would be in the right place.
- Bad, because it is API without a user, and ~hyphenate~ still guesses
  its shape.

*** Option 3: wait, and decide the place now

- Good, because nothing is built that nobody uses, and the design
  question is answered while the reasoning is fresh.
- Bad, because a liblouis user has to ask first.
