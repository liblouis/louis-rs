* PROPOSED ADR-0025: Negation in ~match~ and ~context~ patterns, and no negated strings of more than one character in ~context~
- Deciders :: CE
- Date :: [2026-11-23 Mon]

Technical Story: [[https://github.com/liblouis/louis-rs/issues/79][#79]].
In ~en-ueb-g2.ctb~, ~match !%[_~^] ing - ⠬~ used the "ing" groupsign
at the start of every word but the first. Fixing it raised the question
of what liblouis's ~!~ means. It means different things in ~match~
rules and in ~context~ rules, and neither is a logical complement.

** Context and Problem Statement

*** What negation would be expected to mean

Read as logic, ~!~ is a complement: ~!e~ matches where ~e~ doesn't. On
compound expressions that follows De Morgan's laws:

- ~!(e1|e2)~ is ~!e1 & !e2~: neither alternative matches.
- ~!(e1 e2)~ is "the span is not an ~e1~ followed by an ~e2~", the
  complement of a concatenation, which can't be put together from the
  complements of ~e1~ and ~e2~: ~!e1 !e2~ rejects spans that ~!(e1 e2)~
  accepts, such as an ~e1~ followed by something that isn't an ~e2~.

Both need an intersection (~&~) or a complement of a whole regular
expression. Regular languages are closed under both, but the
constructions are expensive: a complement needs a deterministic
automaton, which can be exponentially larger than the NFA, and an
intersection needs a product automaton. A Pike VM (ADR-0007) runs an
NFA directly, advancing all threads in lockstep one character at a
time, at most one thread per instruction. It has no way to require
two threads to cover the same span, nor to succeed where a thread
fails. That is why Pike-style engines such as RE2 and Rust's ~regex~
offer neither intersection nor negative look-around; only backtracking
engines do, and they give up the linear-time guarantee.

liblouis doesn't implement the complement either. In both of its
pattern languages ~!~ inverts single tests, not expressions, and it
does so in different ways for ~match~ rules and for ~context~ rules.

*** What liblouis does

liblouis has two pattern languages with a ~!~ operator:

- *~match~ rules* (~pattern.c~, ~pattern_check_expression~). The
  manual says "The following expression is negated" (~liblouis.texi~,
  ~match~ opcode). The implementation sets a ~notOperator~ flag, keeps
  the structure of the negated expression (sequences, alternatives,
  repetitions), and inverts each single-character test, which still
  consumes its character. So ~!(ab)~ is "not a, then not b", and
  ~!(a|b)~ is "not a, or not b". ~%[...]~ is one character test whose
  attributes may include the boundary ~^~: ~!%[_^]~ fails at the
  boundary and otherwise takes a character that has none of the
  attributes.
- *~context~, ~correct~ and ~pass2~-~pass4~ rules* (~passDoTest~ in
  ~lou_translateString.c~). The manual says "~!~ reverses the logical
  meaning of the suboperand which follows". ~!~ applies to exactly one
  test and inverts its result. For an attribute or class test with a
  quantifier the inversion is per character, as in ~match~ rules. For
  a string or dots test it applies to the test as a whole, and the
  position still moves on by the length of the string: ~!"ab"~ takes
  any two characters except "ab". It is not a look-ahead: the
  characters are consumed, and with fewer than two characters left the
  test fails, although they aren't "ab" either.

The two meanings differ on the same input: as a ~match~ pattern ~!ab~
rejects "ax" (its "a" is an "a"), while as a ~context~ test ~!"ab"~
accepts it ("ax" is not "ab"). Neither is the De Morgan complement.

*** What louis-rs did

louis-rs compiles both languages to its regexp VM (ADR-0007), which
has no intersection and no negative look-ahead. ~Regexp::negate~
distributes ~!~ over sequences and alternatives and inverts each
character test, which is the ~match~ meaning. Its comments called this
wrong (~!(e1|e2)~ should be ~!e1 & !e2~), and ~doc/Liblouis_Parity.md~
listed it as a gap for both rule kinds.

Two things did not match liblouis:

1. The parser splits ~%[_~^]~ into an alternation of a character class
   and the boundary. Negating that alternation gave "not in the set, or
   not at the boundary", which matches after a space. That is #79.
2. In a ~context~ rule, ~!"ab"~ compiled to "not a, then not b", the
   ~match~ meaning, so it rejected "ax" and "xb", which liblouis
   accepts.

** Decision Drivers

- The VM stays a forward-only, linear-time automaton (ADR-0007).
- Same results as liblouis for every table in its corpus.
- A form louis-rs doesn't support is a parse error, not a silent
  difference (ADR-0018).
- Don't implement a construct no table uses (YAGNI), least of all one
  whose meaning is surprising: it consumes characters, fails near the
  end of the input, and differs from what ~!~ means in ~match~ rules.

** Considered Options

For ~match~ rules:

1. Add an intersection to the VM and make ~!~ a logical complement.
2. Keep ~negate~'s distribution, which is liblouis's meaning, and treat
   a negated ~%[...^]~ as the single character test it is in liblouis.

For a negated string or dots test of more than one character in
~context~ rules:

3. Keep negating it per character (the ~match~ meaning).
4. Spell liblouis's meaning out as alternatives: ~!"abc"~ is
   ~[^a].. | a[^b]. | ab[^c]~.
5. Reject it with a parse error.

** Decision Outcome

Chosen options: 2 and 5.

- ~match~ rules: ~Regexp::negate~ stays as it is, now documented as
  liblouis's ~!~. ~Pattern::Negate(AttributesOrBoundary)~ compiles to
  the negated character class alone: there is a character, and it is
  not in the set. Implemented in ~7358c63~.
- ~context~ rules: a negated string or dots test of more than one
  character is ~ParseError::NegatedLongString~, and the table does not
  load. No table in liblouis's corpus has one; the negated strings in
  ~ru-litbrl.ctb~ and ~th-g1.uti~ are a single character, where all
  three meanings agree.

*** Positive Consequences

- #79 is fixed, along with four tests in liblouis's YAML suite.
- What ~!~ means in each rule kind is written down, and the comments no
  longer call a deliberate behavior a bug.
- louis-rs doesn't commit to the consuming whole-string negation, nor
  silently give a different answer for it.

*** Negative Consequences

- A table outside the corpus with a negated string of more than one
  character in a ~context~ rule does not load. Documented in
  ~doc/Differences_From_Liblouis.md~, with option 4 as the way to
  support it if a table needs it.

** Pros and Cons of the Options

*** 1. Intersection in the VM

- Bad, because it is not what liblouis does: ~!(ab)~ would take "ax".
- Bad, because a Pike VM can't intersect without a product automaton or
  paired threads, which breaks one thread per instruction.

*** 2. Keep the distribution, one test for ~%[...^]~

- Good, because it is liblouis's meaning, and needs no new instruction.

*** 3. Per character in ~context~ rules

- Good, because it is no work.
- Bad, because it silently differs from liblouis: ~!"ab"~ rejects "ax".

*** 4. Alternatives

- Good, because it is exactly liblouis's meaning, in about twenty lines.
- Bad, because no table needs it, and it commits louis-rs to a meaning
  that is surprising and differs from ~match~ rules.

*** 5. Parse error

- Good, because it is a few lines and the difference is visible.
- Bad, because a table using it, if one appears, fails to load until
  option 4 is implemented.
