* ACCEPTED ADR-0020: Implement ~repword~ and ~rependword~ with a constrainer, the trie and ~trace~
- Deciders :: CE
- Date :: [2026-11-06 Fri]

** Context and Problem Statement

~repword~ and ~rependword~ shorten a reduplicated word. In Malay,
~kata-kata~ is written as ~kata~ followed by a sign that stands for
"and again": ~repword - 123456~ gives ~kt=~ (with ~=~ for 123456).
~rependword~ does the same when the repeated part ends a longer word,
and also puts its first dots before that part: with
~rependword - 456,123456~, ~xxxabc-abc~ gives ~xxx:abc=~.

What the rules look for is ~(\w+)-\1~: a run of letters, the
separator, and the same letters again. That is a backreference, which
neither the trie nor louis-rs's regexp VM can express. liblouis
(~isRepeatedWord~ and the code after the replacement in
~lou_translateString.c~) adds three more conditions:

- the repeated part must not start inside a contraction that is
  already in the output: ~aaabc-abc~ stays as it is when ~aaa~ is a
  contraction;
- capitals and emphasis may change only at the start of the word
  and of each repetition:
  ~Abc-Abc~ gives ~^abc^=~;
- further repetitions are swallowed too: ~abc-abc-abc~ gives ~abc=~.

Only ~ms-my-g2.ctb~ uses the two opcodes, with ~-~ as the separator of
both, plus liblouis's ~tests/yaml/repword.yaml~.

** Decision Drivers

- Reproduce liblouis, including the conditions above, each of which a
  case in ~repword.yaml~ tests.
- Keep the per-position analysis precomputed and immutable, like the
  other constrainers.
- No new dependency for one opcode used by one table.

** Considered Options

1. Rewrite the input before translation with a regular expression.
2. A constrainer finds the repetition, the trie the separator, and
   ~trace~ checks what depends on the rules already chosen.
3. Option 2 without the contraction and the indicator checks.

** Decision Outcome

Chosen option: 2, because the backreference is a plain character
comparison that fits a constrainer, and the conditions that depend on
earlier choices fit in ~trace~, the only place that knows them.

How it works, for ~xxxabc-abc~:

1. *Constrainer: the ~\1~.* ~RepwordConstrainer~ looks at every
   separator. It walks outwards while there are letters on both sides,
   which gives the longest possible length (here 3, limited by the
   letters after the ~-~), then tries lengths from that down to 1, and
   keeps the first one where the letters before equal the letters
   after, ignoring case. It flags the positions of that word before
   the separator with ~RepeatedWordEnd~ when a letter comes before the
   word (the ~xxx~), with ~RepeatedWord~ otherwise. The length is not
   stored: it is the number of flagged positions right before the
   separator. Further repetitions after the first, the separator and
   the word again as in ~abc-abc-abc~, are flagged with ~Repetition~.
2. *Trie: the separator.* ~repword~ is inserted as ~-~ followed by a
   letter, ~rependword~ as ~-~ preceded by a letter. Both always hold
   for a real repetition, so they decide nothing. They keep the two
   rules apart from each other and from a plain rule on ~-~ (the trie
   holds one rule per node; ~ms-my-g2.ctb~ includes ~hyphen - 36~), and
   the extra transition outweighs that plain rule.
3. *~trace~: does it apply here?* ~repeated_word~ counts the flagged
   positions before the separator; none means no repetition. The word
   must start where one of the translations already chosen starts,
   not inside a contraction. And no indicator may fall inside the word
   or its repetition, since an indicator marks a change of capitals or
   emphasis. If any
   of this fails, the candidate is dropped and the plain rule on ~-~
   takes over.
4. *~trace~: the output.* The separator is pushed with no output, then
   the indicators at the start of the repetition, then one translation
   that takes the repetitions (~repetitions_end~ adds the ones flagged
   ~Repetition~ as long as no indicator falls in them) and outputs the
   rule's dots.
   ~rependword~ also inserts its first dots before the translations of
   the word.

Both opcodes are forward only, as in liblouis, and switched off in a
word with a ~nocont~ string. The separator is a single character,
which the parser enforces; see "Negative Consequences".

*** Positive Consequences

- ~ms-my-g2.yaml~'s reduplicated words (~kata-kata~, ~barang-barang~)
  come out right.
- All 18 cases in ~repword.yaml~ produce the right dots, including the
  contraction and capitals cases.
- The input analysis is a constrainer like the others; ~trace~ holds
  only what depends on earlier choices.

*** Negative Consequences

- liblouis accepts a separator of several characters; louis-rs
  rejects one with a parse error. No table uses one, and
  reduplication is written with a single hyphen.
- ~repword.yaml~ itself still fails, because its expectations go
  through display rules embedded in the test, which louis-rs ignores
  ([[file:0013-no-inferred-display-entries.org][ADR-0013]]). Its ~inputPos~ and ~outputPos~ checks are therefore
  untested.
- A constrainer flag does not say which separator produced it. With
  one separator character per table in practice, that is moot.

** Pros and Cons of the Options

*** Rewrite the input with a regular expression

Replace ~kata-kata~ with ~kata~ and a marker character that a rule
then translates to the repword dots.

- Good, because it is short to write.
- Bad, because Rust's ~regex~ has no backreferences; it would need a
  new dependency such as ~fancy-regex~.
- Bad, because a text rewrite cannot produce the capital indicator
  before the sign, the dots ~rependword~ inserts before the word, or
  the contraction check, and it needs a position mapping of its own.

*** Constrainer, trie and ~trace~

- Good, because it reproduces every case in ~repword.yaml~.
- Good, because the backreference stays a plain character comparison
  in a precomputed, immutable constrainer.
- Bad, because the opcode is spread over three places, which this
  record exists to tie together.

*** Without the contraction and the indicator checks

Measured: ~ms-my-g2.yaml~ passes the same with either check disabled.

- Good, because about 20 lines less in ~trace~.
- Bad, because it gives up the ~repword.yaml~ cases those checks
  exist for, a silent difference from liblouis on such input.
