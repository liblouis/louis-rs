* ACCEPTED ADR-0024: Model ~_N~ as a look-behind and a look-ahead, and reject it anywhere else
- Deciders :: CE
- Date :: [2026-11-13 Fri]

Technical Story: In liblouis's ~context~ and multipass rules, ~_N~
steps back N characters so that a rule can check its surroundings
without consuming them: ~context _$l["ound"] @46-145a~ contracts
"ound" only after a letter, and ~context "ใคร"!%ToneAndVowels_ ...~
matches "ใคร" only when no vowel or tone mark follows. louis-rs
ignored ~_N~, so such rules matched too much or too little. This
record explains why ~_N~ can't be taken over as it is into louis-rs's
regular-expression matcher, and why it now becomes a look-behind or a
look-ahead. Implemented in ~4d78544~ and ~b2134ba~.

** Context and Problem Statement

The test operand of a ~context~, ~correct~ or ~pass2~-~pass4~ rule is a
sequence of tests that liblouis runs one after the other, each from
where the previous one left off. ~_N~ (~_~ alone is ~_1~) is one of
them: it moves that position back N characters (~pass_lookback~ in
~lou_translateString.c~ and ~lou_backTranslateString.c~), and the rule
fails if it moves before the start of the input. Nothing restricts
where it stands.

louis-rs compiles these tests to its regexp VM ([[file:0007-hand-rolled-regexp-vm.org][ADR-0007]]). Before
~4d78544~ the parser kept ~_N~ as a ~TestInstruction::Lookback~ and the
compiler turned it into ~Regexp::Empty~, i.e. ignored it. That was
wrong in visible ways:

- A trailing ~_N~ was dropped, so a rule without brackets or with a ~*~
  action consumed the character it only meant to check: ~th-g2.ctb~'s
  ~"ใคร"!%ToneAndVowels_~ consumed the character after "ใคร" (most of
  ~th.yaml~'s failures).
- A leading ~_N~ was matched forward as part of the rule, so a ~*~ rule
  consumed its look-behind too (~da-dk-g26.ctb~'s ~_$D[@6-46-6] *~), and
  the rule was found one position early and held as a delayed
  translation, which a rule that consumed across that position hid
  (~context _$l["ound"]~ lost to ~always ar~ in "around").
- ~[$d5]_6~ on "12345" must fail (stepping before the input), louis-rs
  matched it.

So how should ~_N~ be represented, given that the matcher is a
regular-expression engine?

*** Why ~_N~ doesn't fit in a regular expression

A regular expression describes a string read once, left to right: each
piece matches the characters right after the previous piece, and the
match is the concatenation of the pieces. ~_N~ breaks that model:

1. *It is a cursor move, not a pattern.* It matches nothing; it
   rewinds the reading head. Automata (Thompson NFA, Pike VM,
   backtracking alike) only ever advance over the input. There is no
   operator for "go back".
2. *In the middle it means intersection.* ~A _N B~ with ~A~ ending in N
   characters that ~B~ starts on says: those N characters must match
   both the end of ~A~ and the start of ~B~. That is the intersection
   of two languages over an overlapping span, which concatenation
   can't say. It can be emulated by a look-ahead in the middle
   (~A'(?=X)~), but only once you know where ~A~'s last N characters
   start, which needs the next point.
3. *Its meaning depends on widths.* Where ~_N~ lands depends on how
   many characters the tests before it consumed. Over a class with a
   quantifier (~%a.~, ~$l1-3~) the landing point depends on the match,
   and N may land in the middle of a string or a run of dots. The
   pattern can't be compiled without knowing the widths, and with
   variable widths the result isn't one pattern at all.
4. *It interacts with the replacement.* The brackets ~[...]~ mark what
   the rule replaces. A ~_N~ that steps back into or across them makes
   that span ambiguous: the ~[$d5]_6~ of liblouis's own tests steps
   from after the bracket to before it.
5. *It obscures what a rule checks.* ~th-g2.ctb~'s ~!%Ahead_"หมาก"~
   checks that the character at the cursor is not in ~%Ahead~, steps
   back, then reads "หมาก" from the same place. Since ~ห~ is not in ~%Ahead~ the first
   test is a no-op, which the rule does not make apparent.
6. *It hides the rule's position.* Rules are indexed and ranked by the
   characters they start with at the cursor (~passFindCharacters~,
   ~leading_literal_len~, ~Rank~, [[file:0016-rule-selection-by-rank.org][ADR-0016]]). With ~_N~ anywhere, what
   the rule matches "at the cursor" is whatever survives the arithmetic
   of all the ~_N~ and widths before it.

*** How the corpus uses it

Across liblouis's tables ~_N~ appears in exactly two useful shapes,
plus three rules that use it otherwise:

- *Leading*: ~_N~ followed by tests covering exactly N characters, then
  the rule proper: ~_$l["ound"]~, "ound" after a letter. The tests
  after ~_N~ look at what precedes the cursor; that is a look-behind.
- *Trailing*: tests covering N characters at the end, followed by ~_N~:
  ~"ใคร"!%ToneAndVowels_~, "ใคร" not followed by a vowel or tone mark.
  Those characters are checked and given back; that is a look-ahead.
- *Middle*: three rules, ~it-it-comp6.utb~'s two
  ~...%mathnumber_$u.%mathop~ and ~th-g2.ctb~'s ~!%Ahead_"หมาก"~.
  Each was either a no-op or an indirect way of saying something
  simpler, and each has a table fix: [[https://github.com/liblouis/liblouis/pull/2196][liblouis#2196]] and [[https://github.com/liblouis/liblouis/pull/2197][#2197]].
- Three inline tables in liblouis's unit tests (~multipass.yaml~,
  ~multipass-forward.yaml~, ~multipass-backward.yaml~) step back into a
  bracket or before the input on purpose, to test the C code paths.

** Decision Drivers

- The regexp VM stays a forward-only, linear-time automaton
  ([[file:0007-hand-rolled-regexp-vm.org][ADR-0007]]); no backtracking, no general intersection.
- Cover every use in real tables, with the same results as liblouis.
- A rule must not consume what it only checks.
- A context rule is found and ranked where its body starts, not where
  its look-behind starts.
- Prefer an error at table load over silently doing something
  different from liblouis ([[file:0018-parity-or-migration-path.org][ADR-0018]]); fix the tables upstream when
  their ~_N~ is what's wrong (the user co-maintains them).

** Considered Options

1. Keep ignoring ~_N~ (compile it to ~Regexp::Empty~).
2. Emulate liblouis: give the VM a "move back N" instruction and let
   threads rewind.
3. Classify ~_N~ in the parser: a leading ~_N~ becomes a look-behind, a
   trailing one a look-ahead, anything else is a parse error.
4. Option 3, but compile a middle ~_N~ into a look-ahead in the middle
   of the body, where the widths are fixed.

** Decision Outcome

Chosen option: 3, because it covers every real use with constructs a
regular-expression engine already has, and the three rules it rejects
are better fixed in the tables.

- The parser turns the tests and ~_N~s as written into a ~Test~ with a
  ~lookbehind~, a ~body~ and a ~lookahead~ (~Test::from_raw~ in
  ~src/parser/multipass/test.rs~). A ~_N~ before anything that
  consumes characters takes the next tests covering N characters as
  the look-behind; a ~_N~ after the last such test takes the tests
  before it covering N characters as the look-ahead.
- The tests stepped over must have a fixed width: strings, dots, and
  classes or attributes without a quantifier or with an exact count. A
  string or dots that a ~_N~ ends inside is split. Variable tests
  (~#1=1~) consume nothing and go to the body wherever they stand.
  Consecutive ~_~s add up (~__~ is ~_2~).
- Any other ~_N~ (in the middle, into or across a bracket, before the
  input, over a test of open width) is ~ParseError::MisplacedLookback~,
  and the table does not load.
- The compiler consumes only the body. The look-ahead is matched after
  the consumed span and given back. The look-behind is compiled from
  the reversed pattern (~Regexp::reverse~) and run over the reversed
  text before the cursor, exactly as a ~match~ rule's ~pre~ already
  is, so the rule is found at the cursor where its body starts. Its
  weight still counts the look-behind's literals.
- ~Display~ prints the normalized form: look-behind first, then the
  body, then the look-ahead.

*** Positive Consequences

- ~th-g2.ctb~'s trailing ~_N~ rules no longer swallow the next
  character, and ~da-dk-g26.ctb~'s ~*~ rules no longer consume their
  look-behind.
- Context rules with a look-behind are no longer delayed translations,
  so a rule that consumes across their start no longer hides them
  ("around"). Delayed translations remain only for body text before
  ~[~ (see ~TODO.org~ and branch ~context-consume-at-cursor~).
- A rule's look-behind, body and look-ahead are explicit in the data
  structure. ~leading_literal_len~, ~literal_text~ and ~is_literal~ no
  longer do ~_N~ arithmetic.
- The VM is unchanged: no backward moves, no intersection.

*** Negative Consequences

- ~it-it-comp6.utb~ and ~th-g2.ctb~ don't load until [[https://github.com/liblouis/liblouis/pull/2196][liblouis#2196]] and
  [[https://github.com/liblouis/liblouis/pull/2197][#2197]] land. Accepted deliberately: the change went in before them.
- Five tests in liblouis's ~multipass*.yaml~ don't run, as their inline
  tables don't load. A liblouis change testing the same with the two
  supported shapes (e.g. ~_$a["u"]~ at the start of the input) is still
  owed.
- A third-party table with a middle ~_N~ that liblouis accepts fails in
  louis-rs. Documented in ~doc/Differences_From_Liblouis.md~ with the
  error message as the migration path.

** Pros and Cons of the Options

*** 1. Keep ignoring ~_N~

- Good, because it is no work.
- Bad, because it consumes what trailing ~_N~ rules only check, and a
  ~*~ rule consumes its look-behind.
- Bad, because look-behind only worked as a side effect of delayed
  translations, and lost rules a longer translation jumped over.
- Bad, because a ~_N~ before the input never fails.

*** 2. A "move back" instruction in the VM

- Good, because every ~_N~ liblouis accepts would work.
- Bad, because a Pike VM advances all threads in lockstep over the
  input; a thread that rewinds needs its own position, which turns the
  VM into a backtracker and gives up the linear-time guarantee of
  [[file:0007-hand-rolled-regexp-vm.org][ADR-0007]].
- Bad, because it keeps the middle and bracket-crossing forms alive,
  and with them the question of which span is replaced.
- Bad, because rules would still be found at their look-behind and need
  delayed translations.

*** 3. Look-behind and look-ahead, nothing else

- Good, because both are standard regexp constructs, and the
  look-behind reuses the reversed-program machinery of ~match~ rules'
  ~pre~.
- Good, because the shapes are classified once, at parse time, and an
  unsupported shape is reported with its file and line.
- Bad, because it rejects three rules in the corpus and five unit
  tests, which need upstream changes.

*** 4. Option 3 plus a middle look-ahead

~A _N B~ with fixed widths becomes ~A₁(?=A₂B)~ plus whatever of ~B~
reaches past ~A₂~, ~A₂~ being ~A~'s last N characters.

- Good, because it would load ~it-it-comp6.utb~ and ~th-g2.ctb~ as they
  are.
- Bad, because the VM's only look-ahead is the matched but unconsumed
  tail after the ~Consume~ span; one in the middle of a program is the
  intersection again, and would need nested sub-matches.
- Bad, because all three rules it would serve are a no-op or an indirect
  form with a simpler fix in the table.
